### 3.2 Shapley and dividends

Let the zeta matrix be \(Z_{C,A}=1[A\subseteq C]\), for nonempty coalitions. Every game has unique signed dividends
\[
w_A=\sum_{C\subseteq A}(-1)^{|A|-|C|}v(C),\qquad v=Zw.
\]
Write \(H=Z^{-1}\), the integer Möbius matrix. The positive-game assumption is \(w_A\ge0\) for every nonempty \(A\). It is stronger than monotonicity, supermodularity in general, positive observed removal effects, and compatibility of shallow observations [R1–R3].

The focal Shapley value has equivalent forms:
\[
\phi_i(v)=
\sum_{S\subseteq N\setminus\{i\}}
\frac{|S|!(n-|S|-1)!}{n!}\,[v(S\cup\{i\})-v(S)]
=\sum_{A\ni i}\frac{w_A}{|A|}.
\tag{1}
\]
The dividend identity holds for signed games as well as positive games. For an independent score-coordinate implementation, \(\phi_i=c_i^\top v\), where
\[
c_i(C)=
\begin{cases}
\frac{(|C|-1)!(n-|C|)!}{n!},&i\in C,\\
-\frac{|C|!(n-|C|-1)!}{n!},&i\notin C.
\end{cases}
\tag{2}
\]
The empty coordinate has a negative coefficient but contributes zero. Keep exact rational coefficients.

Signed-game shares may be negative despite all coalition scores being in \([0,1]\). Do not clip a share or its interval to \([0,1]\). The entire Shapley vector sums to one; independently chosen coordinate endpoints need not form a feasible joint allocation.

### 3.3 What the supplied theorem establishes

For \(T\subseteq N\setminus\{i\}\), define
\[
m_i(T)=v(N\setminus T)-v(N\setminus(T\cup\{i\})),\qquad
D_i=m_i(\varnothing)=\sum_{A\ni i}w_A.
\]
A complete depth-\(r\) audit supplies **every labeled** \(m_i(T)\) with \(|T|\le r\), not a random selection of that many audits.

For positive focal dividends and exact \(D_i=1\), the source's Theorem 1, pp. 3–4, equations (5)–(8), proves
\[
R_{n,r}=E_{n,r}
=\min_{\deg p\le r}\max_{s=0,\ldots,n-1}
\left|\frac1{s+1}-p(s)\right|.
\tag{3}
\]
For known \(D_i>0\), absolute errors scale by \(D_i\). Under positivity \(D_i=0\) implies \(\phi_i=0\); this implication fails for signed dividends.

For construction, use the binomial basis
\[
q_j(s)=\frac{\binom{n-1-s}{j}}{\binom{n-1}{j}},\qquad
b_j=\binom{n-1}{j}^{-1}\sum_{|T|=j}m_i(T).
\]
If \(p=\sum_{j=0}^r a_jq_j\), the estimator on **raw** marginals is
\(\widehat\phi_i=\sum_j a_jb_j\). Compute a best polynomial by a small LP with \(r+1\) free coefficients and an error variable, imposing both error inequalities on all \(n\) grid points. Do not use a poorly conditioned monomial Vandermonde system for routine floating-point solves.

Why the lower bound handles all labels: normalized focal dividends define a probability law on required-partner subsets; audits are avoidance probabilities. Moment-matched cardinality laws are lifted uniformly within each cardinality, making **every individual labeled audit** identical. Their target values differ by \(2E_{n,r}\). This is stronger than matching average audits and applies against nonlinear estimators. Moment/approximation duality itself is established [R7].

### 3.4 The signed model introduced for this PoC

Define
\[
\nu(v)=\sum_{\varnothing\ne A\subseteq N}\max\{-w_A,0\},
\qquad w=Hv.
\tag{4}
\]
Since \(v(N)=\sum_Aw_A=1\), the total positive mass is \(1+\nu\), and total absolute dividend mass is \(1+2\nu\).

For observed coalitions \(\mathcal K\), exact scores \(y_C\), or independently justified deterministic intervals \([\ell_C,u_C]\), define
\[
\mathcal F_\tau(\mathcal K)=
\{v:\ 0\le v(C)\le1\ \forall C,\ v(\varnothing)=0,\ v(N)=1,\
\ell_C\le v(C)\le u_C\ (C\in\mathcal K),\ \nu(v)\le\tau\}.
\tag{5}
\]
All coalition boxes apply, including unobserved coalitions. The sets are compact convex polytopes. When nonempty, their sharp share intervals are
\[
I_\tau=[L_\tau,U_\tau]
=\left[\min_{v\in\mathcal F_\tau}\phi_i(v),\
\max_{v\in\mathcal F_\tau}\phi_i(v)\right].
\tag{6}
\]
At \(\tau=0\), this is precisely positive completion with score normalization. As \(\tau\) grows the intervals expand. The unrestricted box model, denoted \(I_{\rm box}\), omits only the negative-mass constraint.

This is an explicit new **model adaptation** for this project, using standard LP and sensitivity methods. It does not inherit the source's exact minimax radius for signed games. The parameter depends on the registered player partition: splitting or regrouping contributors can change it. Do not call it a physical measure of substitution.

A good fit to observed scores supplies no upper bound on the true \(\nu\). The minimum compatible negative mass,
\[
\nu_{\min}=\min\{\nu(v): v\text{ satisfies all score boxes and observations}\},
\tag{7}
\]
is a lower bound only. A full exact table identifies the game's actual \(\nu\), but that fact is available to the reference evaluator, not to the partial-observation auditor.

### 3.5 Independent references and a theorem-based sensitivity bound

**Unrestricted box interval.** For each nonempty coalition, combine the observation interval with \([0,1]\); unknown coalitions have \([0,1]\), and full has \([1,1]\). Then
\[
L_{\rm box}=\sum_{c_C\ge0}c_C\ell_C+\sum_{c_C<0}c_Cu_C,\qquad
U_{\rm box}=\sum_{c_C\ge0}c_Cu_C+\sum_{c_C<0}c_C\ell_C.
\tag{8}
\]
This is exact by coordinatewise optimization, requires no LP, and supplies complete endpoint witness tables. Its width for exact observations is \(\sum_{C\notin\mathcal K}|c_C|\). Use this as an independent oracle and a numerical fallback, not as a positive-model certificate.

**Positive completion reference.** Independently formulate the original dividend LP with \(0\le w_A\le1\), \(v=Zw\), all observed rows, and \(\sum_Aw_A=1\). The upper bounds are redundant mathematically but give the verifier an explicit finite box. Minimize/maximize \(\sum_{A\ni i}w_A/|A|\). This must agree with the signed solver at \(\tau=0\). Merely reproducing this established computation is not a novelty claim [R1, R3–R5].

**Conservative extension of the source estimator.** With complete paired depth data, let \(p\) have grid error \(E_{n,r}\) and use raw \(b_j\). By linearity,
\[
|\widehat\phi_i-\phi_i|
\le E_{n,r}\sum_{A\ni i}|w_A|
=E_{n,r}(D_i+2\nu_i)
\le E_{n,r}(D_i+2\tau),
\tag{9}
\]
where \(\nu_i=\sum_{A\ni i}w_A^-\le\nu\).
This is a derivation for this brief. It avoids division by a possibly zero \(D_i\). It is an upper bound, not a proved sharp signed-game minimax formula.

Intersect its interval with \(I_{\rm box}\) when reporting this comparator. The exact all-observation \(I_\tau\) must be contained in every valid such outer interval. Any apparent violation is a bug or an invalid numerical certificate. Smaller LP intervals are expected from stronger use of information; do not present this as a new optimization advance.

For the fixed depth-one comparisons, an exact polynomial avoids floating-point approximation:
\[
p(s)=\frac{n-s}{n}-E_{n,1}
=\left(\frac1n-E_{n,1}\right)+\frac{n-1}{n}q_1(s).
\tag{10}
\]
Use the exact \(E_{n,1}\) in the table. For the initial all-LOO mask only the focal depth-zero pair is complete; its minimax midpoint is \(D_i(n+1)/(2n)\).

### 3.6 Exact three-player sensitivity control

Use labels 1, 2, 3 here and map them consistently to bits in code. Focal player is 1. Observe only empty \(0\), full \(1\), and all three pairs \(2/3\). Singletons are unknown.

Positivity uniquely determines the additive game with each singleton dividend \(1/3\). To see this, summing pair scores and using full score shows that total singleton dividends equal \(1+w_{123}\); positivity and total mass one force all higher dividends to zero.

Write \(x=w_{12},y=w_{13},z=w_{23},t=w_{123}\). The observations imply
\[
x+y+z+2t=0,\qquad
\phi_1-\frac13=-\frac{x+y}{2}-\frac{2t}{3}
=\frac z2+\frac t3.
\]
The two expressions give
\[
-\frac{\nu}{2}\le\phi_1-\frac13\le\frac{2\nu}{3}.
\]
For \(0\le\tau\le1/3\), the following witnesses attain the bounds:

| Dividend | Lower witness | Upper witness |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| \(w_1\) | \(1/3-\tau\) | \(1/3+\tau\) |
| \(w_2,w_3\), each | \(1/3+\tau/2\) | \(1/3-\tau\) |
| \(w_{12},w_{13}\), each | \(\tau/2\) | 0 |
| \(w_{23}\) | \(-\tau\) | \(2\tau\) |
| \(w_{123}\) | 0 | \(-\tau\) |

Their negative mass is exactly \(\tau\), all coalition scores lie in \([0,1]\), all observed scores agree, and both games are monotone. Thus
\[
I_\tau=\left[\frac13-\frac\tau2,\frac13+\frac{2\tau}{3}\right].
\tag{11}
\]
For \(q=1/3,\epsilon=1/20\), the upper endpoint first touches the allowed band's upper boundary at \(\tau=3/40\). The lower endpoint would touch at \(1/10\), so the loss-of-support infimum is \(3/40\). **Support still holds at equality.**

## 4. Implementable algorithm and numerical contract

- \(Hv+z\ge0\).

### 4.2 Exact data and safe numerical certificates

Verify these options on the installed version [R11]. A non-success status is not a usable endpoint. A solver's success flag and small residuals alone do not justify a mathematically guaranteed interval. Implement a lightweight exact verification layer:

1. Retain the rational original LP and finite upper bounds on all variables.
2. Convert finite proposed dual numbers into rationals; also try bounded-denominator reconstruction with denominator limits 1,000, 1,000,000, and 1,000,000,000. Clamping the inequality multipliers to their allowed sign preserves the bound below. Evaluate every candidate by exact arithmetic and retain the strongest valid bound.
3. Produce rigorous outward objective bounds using exact arithmetic.
4. Recover and check an exactly feasible rational score witness; evaluate its scores, dividends, negative mass, observed constraints, and objective exactly.
5. Label an endpoint **certified sharp to tolerance** only when the exact primal/dual bracket has width at most \(10^{-8}\) in normalized-share units. For a threshold use the same absolute \(10^{-8}\) target in negative-mass units.

Here is a dual residual correction that avoids trusting approximate dual feasibility. Write a minimization problem as
\[
\min c^\top X,\quad EX=e,\quad GX\le h,\quad0\le X\le u.
\]
For arbitrary rational \(y\) and rational \(\lambda\ge0\), let
\[
r=c-E^\top y+G^\top\lambda,\qquad
B_{\rm low}=y^\top e-\lambda^\top h+\sum_j u_j\min(0,r_j).
\tag{12}
\]
For every feasible \(X\), \(B_{\rm low}\le c^\top X\). This follows by bounding the Lagrangian over the finite box. Use equality marginals for \(y\), and minus inequality marginals for \(\lambda\), checking the installed SciPy sign convention. Even a poor proposed dual gives a valid, possibly loose bound. For a maximum, apply this calculation to the negative objective and negate its lower bound.

For a feasible witness, first try bounded-denominator rational reconstruction of the proposed score vector, and verify exactly. If necessary, recover a rational vertex from active score bounds, recorded equalities, zero-dividend constraints, an active negative-mass constraint, and any active share-boundary constraint. ### 4.3 Claim decisions

| Inconsistent within tolerance | \(U<a\) or \(L>b\) |

| Model incompatible | No feasible game in the declared model |
| Numerical indeterminacy | The verification bracket does not establish the needed comparison |

Require a verified nonempty model and use outward certified bounds for resolved claims. To certify **unresolved**, verify both a feasible share outside the band and a feasible share inside it; an exact convex mixture of two saved feasible games may supply the inside share. Equivalently, the interval spanned by verified feasible witness objectives must intersect the band and extend outside it. Failure of the outward bounds to certify support or inconsistency is not, by itself, proof of an unresolved identified set. If neither a resolved condition nor the witness condition is established, report numerical indeterminacy separately.

Every status must state its model: positive, negative mass at most a named \(\tau\), or bounded-score-only. A conditional certificate that excludes a true game violating its assumption is not a mathematical false certificate; applying it unconditionally would be an error. Test both the conditional coverage and the software's explicit labeling.

If a monetary amount \(B\) is actually supplied, report \(B[L,U]\). Otherwise report score/share units; do not invent an amount or a realized saving. There are no transfers, withheld fixed compensation, or automated adverse actions.

### 4.4 Loss-of-resolution threshold, including strict boundaries

Calculate this directly by LP, not by interpolating the plotting grid. It is defined only when the positive model is feasible and its interval is resolved.

**Initially supported.** Define
\[
\tau_{\rm loss}=\inf\{\nu(v): v\text{ satisfies the box and observations, }
\phi_i(v)<a\text{ or }\phi_i(v)>b\}.
\tag{13}
\]
Use the exact box interval to check strict reachability.

- If \(L_{\rm box}<a\), solve minimum negative mass subject to \(\phi_i\le a\).
- If \(U_{\rm box}>b\), solve minimum negative mass subject to \(\phi_i\ge b\).
- Take the minimum over reachable branches. If neither branch is reachable, report infinity.

The closed LP reaches the boundary, so its solution is an **infimum for loss**, not a strictly violating witness. In this compact convex model, support holds at the exact finite threshold and fails for every larger budget. For the latter statement, mix a minimum-mass boundary witness with any strictly outside box witness; arbitrarily small positive mixing creates a violating game and adds arbitrarily little mass. Conversely, an outside game attaining the infimum could be mixed with a positive baseline game to reduce its mass while remaining outside, a contradiction.

Record the threshold bracket, boundary witness, and strict outside box witness. For an explicit near-threshold violation, use a rational mixture with allowed extra budget \(h=\max(10^{-6},10^{-4}\max(1,\tau_{\rm loss}))\). Choose a rational mixing weight small enough that the convex upper bound on negative mass is at most the verified threshold upper bound plus \(h\); then verify the actual mass and strict share inequality exactly. Do not call the boundary witness itself a violation.

**Initially inconsistent below the band.** If \(U_0<a\), minimize negative mass subject to \(\phi_i\ge a\), provided \(U_{\rm box}\ge a\). Contact already makes the interval intersect the band, so loss of inconsistency is attained. Otherwise the threshold is infinite.

**Initially inconsistent above the band.** If \(L_0>b\), minimize negative mass subject to \(\phi_i\le b\), provided \(L_{\rm box}\le b\). Again contact is attained.

**Already unresolved or positive-model incompatible.** Report threshold not applicable, preserving the reason and \(\nu_{\min}\). Do not set the threshold to zero to make an aggregate look more fragile.

Expanding \(\tau\) can turn a supported or inconsistent status into unresolved; it cannot turn one resolved status directly into the opposite resolved status, since all previous feasible witnesses remain. Additional observations shrink feasible sets. A threshold can rise if the baseline remains compatible, but an observation that rejects positivity must be reported as model rejection, not as improved robustness.

### 4.5 Pseudocode and failure behavior

~~~text
audit(observations, n, focal, claim, epsilon, tau_grid):
    validate bit masks, frozen identifiers, score domain, and observation intervals
    compute exact Shapley coefficient vector c and exact Mobius/zeta operators
    box_interval, box_witnesses = coordinate_box_extrema(c, observations)
    nu_min = certified_min_negative_mass(observations)
    positive_interval = independently_certified_positive_dividend_LP(observations)
    for tau in tau_grid:
        if certified_lower(nu_min) > tau:
            record MODEL_INCOMPATIBLE; continue
        lo = certified_minimize(c, score_slack_model(observations, tau))
        hi = -certified_minimize(-c, score_slack_model(observations, tau))
        record safe interval, sharpness, witnesses, model-conditional decision
    if positive_interval is certified and resolved:
        loss = reachable_branch_minimizations(
            observations, claim_band, box_interval, positive_interval.status)
        verify boundary and, when applicable, strict-violation witness
    elif positive_interval is mathematically unresolved or model incompatible:
        loss = NOT_APPLICABLE with explicit mathematical reason
    else:
        loss = NUMERICAL_INDETERMINACY or FAILED with verification details
    verify nesting, box containment, and cross-formulation agreement
    append raw results and failure records atomically
    return audit record
~~~

## 5. Smallest sufficient experiment

### 5.1 Baselines and what each establishes

All partial-information methods see the identical mask and scores. All may cache those observations. Charge unique raw coalition scores once per branch and record shared preprocessing separately.

| Method | Role | Necessary comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Independent positive-dividend LP | Closest established reference and positivity-only ablation | Must equal the signed LP at \(\tau=0\); shows which decisions rely on positivity. |
| Signed sensitivity LP | Proposed diagnostic adaptation | Sharp endpoints and thresholds, with exact witnesses and outward verification. |
| Coordinatewise box bounds | Simple credible reference and independent endpoint oracle | Bounds every signed interval; determines strict reachability without an LP. |
| Source polynomial estimator, extended by (9) | Transparent conservative control | Use only complete available depth families; no claim of superiority over sharp completion. |
| Exact full table: permutation Shapley and Möbius Shapley | Correctness reference | Independent agreement; conditional containment whenever the true game satisfies the declared budget. |
| One fixed extra focal-singleton observation and one fixed random observation | Information controls, not a learned query policy | Compare their changes under equal one-score budgets; preserve the same base game and initial data. |

Leave-one-out is recorded as the full-coalition marginal \(D_i\), with that label. It is not treated as an estimate of the same target without qualification.

Do not implement a broad permutation-sampling competition here. The chosen contribution concerns identified sets and assumption relaxation, not Monte Carlo error or sampling efficiency. Sampling methods are substantial prior art [R9], and would be mandatory in a later audit-cost superiority study; they do not compute this negative-mass frontier. Similarly, fixed fees and milestone contracts remain business alternatives, but no partner decision exists to compare them empirically. Exhaustive evaluation remains a serious production alternative at 16–64 coalitions; this technical study makes no claim to beat its real execution cost.

### 5.2 Mandatory exact controls

Implement these before generating the confirmatory cohort. Several checks may share one game; do not create separate infrastructure for each.

1. **Three-player frontier:** Section 3.6 at \(\tau=0,1/100,3/40,1/10,1/3\). Verify exact endpoints, monotonicity of witnesses, boundary semantics, and positive-model rejection after the counterexample's focal singleton is observed.
2. **Four-player indistinguishable positive worlds:** focal A; world 1 has dividends \(1/3\) on AB, AC, AD; world 2 has \(2/3\) on A and \(1/3\) on ABCD. Complete focal depth-one scores plus empty give positive interval \([1/2,3/4]\); A alone collapses it to \(1/2\) or \(3/4\).
3. **Additive and unanimity games:** recover exact Shapley values; an all-or-nothing grand-coalition requirement yields \(1/n\) per player. Check complete observations collapse feasible intervals, provided the negative budget is sufficient.
4. **Duplicates/substitutes:** \(v(S)=1[S\cap\{A,B\}\ne\varnothing]\), with any remaining players genuine dummies in the full reference. Shares A=B=\(1/2\), other shares zero; pair dividend \(w_{AB}=-1\). Test positivity rejection when revealing enough scores, without assuming hidden dummy equalities.
5. **Harmful intermediate contribution:** with distinct A,H,R, set \(v(S)=1[A\in S]\cdot1[H\notin S\ \text{or}\ R\in S]\). It has full score one and a negative Shapley value for H. Verify that signed intervals preserve it and no output clips or renormalizes it.
6. **Input/model failures:** zero total, inconsistent duplicate records, out-of-range scores, unknown bundle ID, missing score, changed integration/test hash. Missing observations remain unknown. Input-version mismatch invalidates the comparison rather than creating a score of zero.
7. **Unsupported structure:** generate a high-order unanimity interaction; verify an unjustified interaction-size cap is not silently imposed. Verify \(D_i=0\) is not used to force zero signed Shapley.
8. **Identity and joint-allocation controls:** split one substitute identity in a duplicate fixture and show the declared game and allocation can change; do not claim identity immunity. Use separate valid endpoint witnesses to demonstrate why independently selected coordinate extremes need not sum to one.
9. **Numerical and information controls:** permute labels and recover permuted answers; poison all hidden scores while retaining the visible mask and require identical scientific output under a fixed deterministic tie convention, excluding timing, record IDs, timestamps, ledger counters, and logging metadata; perturb a proposed primal/dual certificate and require the verifier to reject it or safely widen its bound. If equivalent tied witnesses differ, their independently verified conclusions and objective brackets must still agree.
10. **Bounded observations:** widen one exact observation into a justified test interval and verify identified sets cannot shrink. This tests deterministic interval handling, not statistical coverage.
11. **Large signed cancellation:** use four players with value one on odd-cardinality coalitions and on the full coalition, and zero on the remaining coalitions. Its dividends are 1 on each singleton, -2 on each pair, 4 on each triple, and -7 on the full set, so \(\nu=19\) and every Shapley share is \(1/4\). With the full table observed, budget 18 must be rejected and budget 19 must give the exact share. This checks that the API does not mistakenly cap negative mass at one.

Independently compute reference Shapley values by enumerating all \(n!\le720\) permutations and by exact dividend division. Use direct subset summation and an independent fast zeta/Möbius transform round trip. Check efficiency across all players. A pointwise theorem test is not a proof of its universal guarantee; retain the derivation.

### 5.3 Fixed synthetic generators

Generate rational Boolean-requirement games. These are mechanism probes, not “realistic accepted deliveries.” The generators intentionally include complements, substitutes, and harmful partial integrations. Each requirement is zero at empty and one at full; weights are positive and normalized once, so every game has the required endpoint scores.

For every game, choose focal \(i=0\), synthetic claim \(q=1/n\), and \(\epsilon=1/20\) **before computing any coalition scores**. This equal-share claim is a controlled scenario, not a measured buyer practice. Retain all claims whether true, false, unresolved, or model incompatible.

Use four families:

| Family | Exact generation |
| --- | --- |
| ADD: additive contributions | Draw one integer weight independently from 1 through 9 for each player; \(v(S)=\sum_{j\in S}a_j/\sum_j a_j\). |
| AND: positive complementarity | Create 12 requirements. For each, choose size uniformly from 1 through n, then a uniformly sampled subset A of that size; its outcome is \(1[A\subseteq S]\). |
| ALT: alternate implementations | Create 12 requirements: six AND requirements as above; six alternatives. For each alternative choose distinct a,b uniformly, choose a common core C by sampling a size uniformly from 0 through n−2 and then that many of the remaining players; set A=C∪{a}, B=C∪{b}, and outcome \(1[A\subseteq S\ \text{or}\ B\subseteq S]\). |
| REPAIR: conflict repaired by another bundle | Create six AND requirements and six repair requirements. For each repair choose distinct h,r uniformly; choose a nonempty A from the other n−2 players by a uniform size and uniform subset; outcome \(1[A\subseteq S]\cdot1[h\notin S\ \text{or}\ r\in S]\). |

For each non-ADD family, draw each of the 12 integer weights independently from 1 through 9 and divide the weighted outcome sum by their total. Preserve duplicate requirements; they are not rejected or resampled. ALT is monotone but can have negative dividends. REPAIR can be nonmonotone while the full accepted delivery scores one. The explicit unanimity expansions of ALT and REPAIR each introduce one negative term per affected requirement; their actual negative mass must still be computed from the entire table because cancellation can occur. Every cohort truth consequently has \(\nu\le1\). This is a deliberate limited-substitution/conflict regime, not coverage of arbitrary high-cancellation games; the separate \(\nu=19\) control tests that numerical boundary without contributing to cohort prevalence summaries.

Use the standard-library pseudorandom generator with persisted complete manifests. For family index f=0,1,2,3 in the table order and player count n, use seeds:

- Development: \(100000+1000n+100f+k,\ k\in\{0,1\}\).
- Confirmation: \(900000+1000n+100f+k,\ k\in\{0,1,2,3\}\).

Generate each requirement's labels and subset before drawing its weight, in the listed requirement order; persist the resulting requirement list so reproducibility does not depend solely on a library's future random behavior. Use a separately seeded generator for the random-query control, seed equal to the instance seed plus 5000000. It must not consume or depend on hidden scores.

This gives 24 development and 48 confirmation base games, balanced over 12 size/family cells. A base game is the independent unit. Its masks, \(\tau\) values, witnesses, and repeated measurements are dependent conditions, not extra independent deliveries.

### 5.4 Observation masks and sensitivity settings

For each base game use these four masks:

- \(K_0=\{\varnothing,N\}\cup\{N\setminus\{j\}:j\in N\}\): n+2 scores.
- \(K_1=K_0\cup\{N\setminus\{i,j\}:j\ne i\}\): 2n+1 scores, completing focal depth one.
- \(K_{\rm single}=K_1\cup\{\{i\}\}\): 2n+2 scores.
- \(K_{\rm random}=K_1\cup\{C_{\rm random}\}\): 2n+2 scores, with the additional nonempty unobserved coalition uniformly chosen from the complement of \(K_1\) using the separate seed.

The random control may equal the focal singleton; retain that coincidence rather than resampling. These are separate branches with a shared prefix, not a single trajectory that gives one comparator free observations from another.

The primary sensitivity grid is
\[
\tau\in\{0,\ 1/100,\ 1/20,\ 1/10,\ 1/3,\ 1\}.
\]
Calculate thresholds independently of this grid. After frozen auditor outputs are saved, the reference evaluator may additionally check containment at the exact true \(\nu(v)\). That value is an oracle-only correctness check, not an input to claim selection, query selection, or the normal audit.

Generate a full exact reference table, but expose to the auditor only the selected records and generic metadata: n, focal, q, epsilon, score bounds, and hashes. The auditor does not receive the family, requirements, seed, full-table path, or true negative mass. A separate evaluator joins those fields after the frozen audit record is written. The poisoning control in Section 5.2 verifies this interface separation.

No adaptive query optimizer, learned prior, interaction cap, or permutation truncation is included. These would change the project.
